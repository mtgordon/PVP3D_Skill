- **The tie stiffness does not limit convergence here**: 1 N/mm holds the tied tissue to ~0.01-0.02 mm and converges

**`cutback` has never done anything in these models.** FEBio's `FETimeStepController::Retry()` (FECore source):
`if (m_nretries == 0) m_ddt = dt / (m_maxretries + 1); dtn = (m_naggr == 0) ? dt - m_ddt : dt * m_cutback;`. With
`aggressiveness` 0 (every model here) each retry takes dt0/21 off the step (max_retries 20), a linear walk down; the
`cutback` value (0.25 here, 0.5 in the 3-surface block) is read only with `aggressiveness` 1. The logs show it: L6_blk's
retries at t = 0.69926, 0.699057, 0.698854 ... step down by 0.000203 = 0.00426 / 21. A real cutback (aggressiveness 1:
halve the step at each failure) was then tested in batch 22 (L8_aggr): no measurable change with the line search on.

- The cross-section material brings the USL further from the connectors (60-62 % of their median opening, against
  67-79 % with the strip fit); CL barely changes (93 vs 97 %). Why (discussed with the user): the real loft strains its
  short part more (u / its own length), so with a stiffening material the short end pulls harder (CL at u = 10 mm: 31 %

  material can copy the connectors exactly: a connector's force depends on its elongation only, not its length.
- Tissue density on the CL/USL lofts: the same solution, but more failed attempts (37 vs 25) and slower: not free here
  (one run each).

**The slower ramp (user's idea, 20:30).** In a dynamic run the speeds scale with 1/(ramp time) and the inertial forces
with 1/(ramp time)^2, so a slower ramp calms the wall without adding dissipation. Abaqus ramps over exactly 1 s
(`*Dynamic, Explicit`, time 1.0, `Amp-1` SMOOTH STEP 0 -> 1, bulk viscosity 0.06 / 1.2), and FEBio copies that: a slower
ramp is a route to the loaded state, not the like-for-like timing (keep 1 s for the U/V comparison).
`L13_ramp3` (`tools/build_batch27.py`, started 20:33, 16 threads): L11_stab with Amp-1 over 0-3 s, the settle damping's
switch-on moved to 3.0-3.15 (still off during the ramp), step_size and dtmax x3 (the same number of steps); compare
with the 1 s runs at t(ramp3) = 3 t.

**What the slow ramp showed.** It got further in load than the 1 s run (81 % vs 65 %), but at the same load its LA is
~2.5x larger: **the 1 s dynamic runs lag the load** (inertia), so their mid-ramp states understate the loaded
deformation, and when the load is followed closely the LA deforms far more and finally snaps through in its PCM/PRM part.
Open question: are the 1 s runs' t = 1 states at equilibrium (a session with the full-load model with the stab springs
found them close), or lagging too, e.g. the clean all-loads-1/3 run L12_stabdamp_p3 (LA 6.3 mm median at t = 1)? A held
load (extend it to t ~2 with the load constant) answers that cheaply.
